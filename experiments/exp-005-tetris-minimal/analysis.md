# exp-005-tetris-minimal — Analysis

## Objective

Test whether explicit scope reduction in the seed enables Tetris to succeed.
Hypothesis: reducing required functions from ~8 to ~4 fits in the 7B token budget.

## Runs

| Run | Model | Cycles | Verdict | Functional | Visual | Seed bytes | Compression |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| run-001 | Qwen7B Q5_K_M | 2 | ✓ ALL_COMPLETE | FAIL* | 7.0/10 | 99 | 0.0324 |

*Likely false negative — Critic says ALL_COMPLETE, visual 7.0/10, executor selector mismatch.

## Key findings

Scope reduction works: the model successfully implements falling pieces and left/right movement in 2 cycles. But the seed grows from 28 bytes (full Tetris, fails) to 99 bytes (minimal Tetris, succeeds). Compression ratio degrades from theoretically ~0.005 to 0.0324 — 7x worse.

This is the central tension of the research. The model's decompression capacity is fixed. When the artifact exceeds that capacity, the human must add information to the seed. But adding information to the seed directly increases its Kolmogorov complexity, defeating the purpose of compression.

The executor false negative (game_area_present fails) suggests the DOM structure generated by minimal Tetris doesn't match our selector patterns. The game is visually complete (7.0/10) but the executor can't verify it programmatically. Selector broadening needed.
